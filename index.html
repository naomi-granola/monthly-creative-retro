Glossary — what each vp- tag means
Notepad — The capture itself — records the conversation into notes you can trust, jotting optional. Merges the old vp-notetaking tag.
Be present — The emotional payoff of being fully in the room — not splitting focus between listening and writing.
No bots — No bot joins the call; the meeting stays between the people in it (human, not stealth).
Around the meeting — The whole before/during/after arc in one tool — "around the meeting, not in it."
Prep — Walking in prepared before — briefs, "walk in ready," pulling context before you join.
Follow-ups — After the meeting — follow-up drafts, client-ready summaries, speed-to-recap.
Recall — Retrieving/querying what was said — chat across past meetings, "what did I miss?"
Integrations — Works with your other tools — Slack, Notion, CRM, MCP/API ("feeds your stack").
Cross-platform — Works anywhere — any meeting tool, phone, in-person, mobile, offline.
Privacy — Data security / "just for me" — sensitive 1:1s, saved on your device.
Social proof — Others vouch — testimonials, referrals, a real shared summary. Was vp-trust.
The heaviest-funded messages are the least efficient. Be present and Notepad take the most budget (top: £387,206) and buy meetings at £64–£74, while smaller-budget Integrations (£50), Recall (£51), Prep (£58), No bots (£59) run cheaper. Case to shift test budget toward the under-funded angles. (Cost per first valid meeting, the goal metric — same event the weekly report ranks on.)

Does the Instagram Reels skew hurt meeting quality?
No — the creative leading on Instagram Reels converts just as well. C119_g1_v1 · joshbaraket (Gravitas, Partnership) is the Top-10 creative whose lead placement is Instagram Reels (67%) rather than Feed — its CP-FVM is £83 and its CPC is £1.19, against a Feed-led range of £68–£161 CP-FVM among the rest of the Top 10. If a Reels-heavy delivery mix were diluting conversion quality, this ad would show it first — instead it sits inside (or below) the Feed-led range, so Meta is finding an efficient audience there, not just a cheap one. Worth deliberately testing more creative into off-Feed placements rather than treating the mix as something to correct back toward Feed.

Read: the themes have moved from keyword-trigger noise to objections — consent, platform, price, and build-it-yourself. None of these appeared in the July read, and none are about note quality. Two are directly actionable: the misspelling in the top spender, and the fact that an Apple Watch ad is drawing Android questions.
Caveat on coverage: this is 7 comments read in full from one post. Meta creates a separate per-ad DCO post for each ad instance, so a creative's comments are fragmented across many posts — a second post checked for C119_g1_v1 had zero despite the creative showing 41. A full theme read means walking the posts ad-by-ad; there is no comments endpoint in the Ads API to do it in bulk.

Signals

The account is a duplication machine. 2,816 active ad instances resolve to just 568 unique creatives — 90% of spend sits in only 144 of them.
New taxonomy is landing — statics lead volume. 285 creatives launched this period, led by Siha (129) and Newform (80), with statics outpacing video 128 to 97.
Work-email share, not volume, separates the campaigns. WorkEmail Scaling · US returns 31% work accounts at £151 a meeting; Desktop Testing · US returns 57% at £46.
Partnership is the primary spend vehicle. Whitelisted/creator video is £532,107 (36% of format spend) — ahead of brand static — with Siha the biggest production source.
The heaviest-funded messages are the least efficient. Be present, Notepad take the most budget but buy meetings at £64–£74, while Integrations, Recall, Prep, No bots run cheaper.
Legacy still holds a chunk of the money. Untagged pre-taxonomy winners are 15% of spend — the tagged-archetype migration is now carrying the majority of budget.
